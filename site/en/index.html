Blurt’s accuracy
CER 2.7%Chinese and mixed Chinese–English, 20 clips with product names. 90–97% of the names right.
CER 26.7%The same model on a harder set, 6 voices with room noise added. 38% of the names right.
WER 1.9%English, the speech model alone. 7 product-feedback sentences, 2 voices each.
CER 10.8%16–24 GB Macs, the 0.6B model with polish, Chinese and mixed. 86% of names right (measured on v0.8).

None of the other four publish Chinese figures. English: Wispr Flow about 3.9% and Typeless about 7.9% (the same article also says about 20%), from VoiceDash. Aqua Voice publishes an average WER of 6.24% on the OpenASR leaderboard.

Blurt’s numbers were measured on one Apple M5 Max (128 GB), with speech made by macOS say as the input. That is cleaner than a real microphone, so the error rates are optimistic. Real microphones, accents and smaller Macs were not measured. 0.39 s is after 60 s of speech, the median of 3 takes; 42–54 s takes with live text on took 0.51–1.55 s, median 0.64 s. Requirements, models and permissions
System
macOS 13 or later. Dictation and the recommended speech models need Apple silicon. Windows and Linux are coming soon. No phone app.
Models
Qwen3-ASR 1.7B with Gemma 4 E2B on 32 GB Macs and up: about 5.9 GB to download, about 4.2 GB of memory in use; the 0.6B model on 16–24 GB: about 4.4 GB, 2.8 GB of memory. Nothing downloads until you press Download. Idle models unload after 10 minutes, sooner when memory runs short.
Languages
Dictation was tested in Chinese, English and the two mixed. The speech model lists 30 languages; the others weren’t measured. The app is in English and Simplified Chinese.
Words
Add project names, libraries and teammates. A word you fix twice is learned, with Undo. Words are hints, never replacements.
History
Every dictation stays in Dictation History for 30 days, with Copy. The mic opens on the key press, so the first word isn’t cut off.
Permissions
Microphone; Accessibility, to paste and hear fn; Input Monitoring, for the other keys; Screen Recording, asked the first time you snap or record
Typeless or Wispr Flow installed
They use fn too. Blurt tells you when it sees one, so you can quit it or move Blurt to Right Option.

The full user guide

Download for MacFree, macOS 13 or later

Or have your agent install it:

$curl -fsSL https://blurt.agihunt.info/install.sh | sh

Give it to Claude Code or Codex, or paste it into a terminal. It downloads Blurt, puts it in Applications and opens it. You turn on the microphone and Accessibility yourself, in the setup window.

Six keys to know

  • fnTap to talk, tap again to stop
  • fnfnDouble-tap: stop and send
  • While talking: make it a list
  • ⌥ASnap, draw on it, say it
  • ⌥VVoice note
  • ⌥RRecord

The other keys (re-format, pause, review…) are in the user guide. You can change each one in Settings.
