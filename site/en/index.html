Accuracy

Blurt’s accuracy, own tests
CER 2.7%Chinese and mixed Chinese–English, 20 clips with product names. 90–97% of the names right.
CER 26.7%The same model on a harder set: 6 voices, room noise added. 38% of the names right.
WER 1.9%English, the speech model alone: 7 product-feedback sentences, 2 voices each.
CER 10.8%16–24 GB Macs: the full dictation path with the 0.6B model and polish, Chinese and mixed; 86% of names right (measured on v0.8).
The other four, and how these were measured

None of the other four publish Chinese figures. Their English figures come from different tests: Wispr Flow about 3.9% and Typeless about 7.9% (the same article also says about 20%), both from VoiceDash, a competing vendor; Aqua Voice publishes its own average WER of 6.24% on the OpenASR leaderboard.

Qwen3-ASR 1.7B, used on Macs with 32 GB or more; 16–24 GB Macs use the smaller 0.6B model. Blurt’s numbers are its own tests on one Apple M5 Max (128 GB), with speech made by macOS say fed in as the microphone: cleaner than a real mic, so error rates are optimistic. Real microphones, accents and smaller Macs were not measured. Prices and policies were checked on 2026-10-25 and change.

Snap it. Say it. Drag it in.

Snaps and voice notes wait under the notch as cards, several at once. Drag one into Claude, ChatGPT, Slack or WeChat: the picture goes in, then your words in the same box. Drop it in a terminal and Claude Code gets both as files.

⌥⇧V

Voice notePress it and talk; pauses to think are fine. Press it again to finish, up to 10 minutes. The words show under the notch as you talk, and the note becomes a card, like a snap.

Where a card lands
Claude, ChatGPT, Slack, WeChat, Feishu, NotionThe picture as an attachment, then your note in the same message box.
Terminal, iTerm2, Ghostty, WarpThe paths of the picture and a Markdown note, so Claude Code or Codex reads both. Nothing runs in the shell.
Cursor, VS CodeThe picture; your note goes on the clipboard for ⌘V.
A click on a cardCopies it for ⌘V. ⌘-click several to copy them together. Cards you dragged out stay findable for 7 days.

Record it. Get items. Hand them off.

Click through your product and say what you see. The coding agent on your computer turns the recording into items, each with a frame.

  1. ⌥⇧R

    Pick a window or an area. Use it and talk.What you say is transcribed while you record. Press ⌥⇧R again, or Finish at the notch, to stop.

  2. ⌃drag

    Hold ⌃ and drag to circle what’s wrong.The circle is in the frame; on screen it fades after 3 s.

  3. Finish

    Your coding agent writes the items.Bugs get repro steps, actual and expected, and the likely code; ideas keep the moment that sparked them.

52items

One 5-minute walkthrough became 52 items, each with what you said and when you said it.2

It transcribes while you record, and it’s ready about a minute after you stop.1

1 Measured once in Blurt’s README FAQ: a 5-minute, 12-item recording was ready to review 51 s after Finish. Organising uses your own agent: three real 7–20 minute sessions cost about $2–2.6 each at API prices.

2 A teammate’s 5-minute walkthrough; how long that one took to be ready was not recorded.

No Blurt server. No account.

Stays on your Mac:recognition and polish for dictation, snap notes and voice notes; a dictation’s audio (deleted after transcription unless you keep it); about 500 characters around the cursor, used for that dictation only, never from password fields.

Goes online only for:model downloads and update checks; a cloud speech key you add yourself, for recordings; and your agent organising a recording, which sends the transcript and a few frames to its provider.

Questions

Which models, and how big?

Open-source models, downloaded once during setup and run locally from then on. 32 GB and up: Qwen3-ASR 1.7B for speech and Gemma 4 E2B for polish, about 5.9 GB together. 16–24 GB: Qwen3-ASR 0.6B with Gemma 4 E2B, about 4.4 GB. 8 GB Macs polish with Qwen3 1.7B. Idle models unload after 10 minutes.

Which languages?

Dictation was tested in Chinese, English and mixed Chinese and English. The speech model lists 30 languages; the others weren't measured. The app is in English and Simplified Chinese.

What if a take gets lost?

Every dictation stays in Dictation History for 30 days, with Copy. The mic opens on the key press, so the first word isn't cut off.

Already using Typeless or Wispr Flow?

They listen to Fn too. Blurt tells you when it sees one, so you can quit it or move Blurt to Right Option.

Windows and Linux?

Mac only today. Windows and Linux are coming soon. There is no phone app.

Which permissions does it need?

Microphone; Accessibility, to paste and to hear Fn; Input Monitoring, for other keys; Screen Recording, asked the first time you snap or record.
